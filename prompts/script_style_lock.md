# SCRIPT DNA STYLE LOCK — CANONICAL SAMPLE RHYTHM

These rules are not optional. They define how ARCH A and ARCH C scripts must sound. The Script DNA architecture remains the governing structure, but these canonical samples define the cadence, compression, transitions, and level of specificity.

## Core cadence for ARCH A

1. OPEN WITH ONE LONG SYMPTOM-STACK SENTENCE.
   - Start with "If your..." or "If you're..."
   - Chain 4 concrete, relatable symptoms/frustrations with commas and "and".
   - Do not stop after 2 symptoms.
   - End the opening sentence with a short diagnosis/reframe such as "your routine might be missing...", "your skin needs...", "you're fighting the wrong battle", or another product-appropriate equivalent.

2. CAUSE / REFRAME MUST START DIRECTLY.
   - Usually begin with "Because..."
   - One or two sentences maximum.
   - Explain the mechanism/reason using ONLY supplied facts.
   - Do not pad with generic wellness language.

3. OBJECTION PRE-EMPT MUST HIT HARD AND FAST.
   - Prefer "And no, [common alternative] isn't..." or "And no, [thing they tried] isn't doing what you think..."
   - Attack the inadequate alternative, never the viewer.

4. THREE DISTINCT BENEFIT BEATS.
   - Exactly three short beats whenever source facts support three.
   - Ingredient/feature FIRST, benefit SECOND.
   - Each beat should feel like its own sentence or crisp clause.
   - Reusing the same documented ingredient for two different documented functions is allowed when necessary.
   - Never merge all benefits into one vague paragraph.
   - Preserve compliant hedging: "to help support", "can help support", "to help improve the appearance of", etc.

5. VILLAIN / ALTERNATIVE BEAT.
   - Prefer "But don't..." or "But don't go..."
   - One sentence.
   - Attack wasted effort, messy routines, random single-form buying, quick fixes, or another documented/appropriate alternative.
   - No first-person filler before this point.

6. PRODUCT REVEAL MUST BE DECISIVE.
   - Brand/product + strong construction verb: "combined", "packed", "put", "kept", "built", "made", "designed".
   - Explain what the product actually is and why its format/formula is useful.
   - Do not say "the listing shows", "the product page says", "trust signals", or other meta language.

7. DIFFERENTIATOR = SHORT, CONCRETE DETAILS.
   - Use 1-3 compact sentences/fragments.
   - Examples of the desired rhythm: texture/application, number of forms, capsule count/supply, clean-formula attributes, ease of use, format, included pieces.
   - Only use facts actually supplied.

8. CREDENTIAL / OFFER BEAT IS OPTIONAL AND FACT-ONLY.
   - If there is a concrete documented trust signal, use it plainly.
   - Never write vague phrases like "strong trust signals from shoppers".
   - Never invent sales counts, clinical status, discounts, shipping, awards, or popularity.
   - If no concrete credential is supplied, omit this beat rather than filling space.

9. CALLBACK + CTA.
   - Prefer "So if you're tired of [mirror the opening problem], I recommend trying this/these. Tap the orange cart down below."
   - Keep it short.

## BANNED STYLE BEHAVIORS

Do NOT write any of the following unless literal product facts force a close equivalent:
- "I also like that..."
- "One thing I noticed..."
- "What I like about this..."
- "the listing shows..."
- "strong trust signals..."
- "shoppers seem to..."
- "this fits into your routine" as generic filler
- "you want a form/product that..." as a vague thesis sentence
- "the real issue is often..." when a direct "Because..." reframe is available
- broad summaries such as "supports overall wellness" when specific supplied benefits exist
- repeated explanation of the same benefit using different words
- corporate ad language, listicle language, or review-summary language

## POV LOCK
- Second-person dominates the script.
- Do not use "I", "me", or "my" in the hook, reframe, objection, or benefit beats.
- First-person is reserved for the final recommendation/CTA and, only when natural, the villain beat.

## COMPRESSION LOCK
- Every sentence must perform a Script DNA beat.
- If a sentence can be deleted without losing a symptom, cause, objection, benefit, villain, reveal, differentiator, credential, callback, or CTA, delete it.
- No filler added merely to hit word count. Expand with concrete sensory/use/differentiator details from the supplied facts instead.

## CANONICAL SAMPLE PATTERNS

Sample pattern — magnesium:
"If you're tossing and turning every night and can't get deep sleep, your body isn't recovering the way it should, you feel on edge and wound up but can't seem to relax, and you just aren't bouncing back when you wake up, your routine might be missing vital magnesium. Because [direct factual reframe]. And no, [weak alternative] isn't cutting it. [Ingredient/form] to help support [benefit]. [Ingredient/form] to help support [benefit]. And [ingredient/form] to help support [benefit]. But don't go [villain behavior]. [Brand] combined [documented formula] into [format]. [Concrete differentiators]. So if you're tired of [callback], I recommend trying this. Tap the orange cart down below."

Sample pattern — skincare:
"If your [symptom 1], [symptom 2], [symptom 3], and [symptom 4], your skin needs [reframe]. Because [direct factual cause]. And no, [common alternative] isn't [fixing it]. [Ingredient] to help [benefit]. [Ingredient] to help [benefit]. And [ingredient] to help [benefit]. But don't [villain]. [Brand] packed/put/kept [formula] into [format]. [Texture/use/differentiator]. So if you're tired of [callback], I recommend trying this. Tap the orange cart down below."


# ARCH C STYLE LOCK — CANONICAL DAY-BY-DAY RHYTHM

ARCH C is not just “mention a few dates.” It must feel like a progressive story with an immediate-use scene, escalating milestones, a sharp villain pivot, and a decisive product reveal.

## Core cadence for ARCH C

1. CURIOSITY HOOK MUST USE THE CANONICAL QUESTION SHAPE.
   - Prefer: “What would actually happen to your [area/body concern] if you [used/took] the viral [product type] for [realistic timeframe] straight?”
   - Keep it to one sentence.
   - Use “viral” only as social framing, never as proof of efficacy.

2. DAY ONE MUST FEEL LIKE A REAL FIRST USE.
   - Start explicitly with “Day one,”
   - Show the actual action: chew, spray, wash, apply, take, mix, etc.
   - Include one concrete sensory/use detail when supplied: flavor, cooling/tingling feel, texture, capsule count, rinse/no-rinse, etc.
   - State that nothing visibly changes yet or the user feels little/no immediate difference.
   - Include a short skepticism beat: “you wonder...” / “you still see...” / “nothing looks different yet.”
   - Then pivot with “But...” into the documented formula/ingredient setup.
   - Do not promise an immediate result.

3. FIRST MILESTONE = WEEK ONE OR WEEK TWO.
   - Use whichever is more natural for the product/source facts; do not force Week One if Week Two fits better.
   - This milestone must be subtle and observable, not dramatic.
   - Use hedges: “may start,” “might notice,” “may not feel as...,” “may look...”
   - End with a short ingredient/mechanism explanation when supported: “That’s the [ingredient] starting to help support...” or “That’s because...”

4. DAY THIRTY = THE SHIFT / CLICK BEAT.
   - Prefer: “Day thirty, this is where things may start to shift.” or “...start to click.”
   - Give 2-3 concise, product-appropriate changes.
   - Keep all efficacy language hedged and grounded in supplied claims.
   - Avoid vague filler such as “overall wellness may improve.”

5. DAY SIXTY / ENDPOINT = STRONGEST PAYOFF, STILL HEDGED.
   - Prefer: “Day sixty, this is usually where people start...” / “...where people realize...” / another close equivalent.
   - Give 2-3 concrete outcomes tied back to the hook problem.
   - Even here, use “may,” “might,” “may look,” “may feel,” unless a stronger exact claim is documented and compliant.
   - Do not present guaranteed transformations.

6. VILLAIN PIVOT MUST BE SHARP.
   - Prefer: “But here’s where most people go wrong.” or “But here’s what most people get wrong.”
   - Follow immediately with one sentence describing the inferior alternative: random single-ingredient products, mismatched products, harsh formula, filler-heavy version, etc.
   - Attack the alternative, never the viewer.

7. PRODUCT REVEAL MUST LAND IMMEDIATELY AFTER THE VILLAIN.
   - Brand/product + construction verb: “uses,” “combined,” “built,” “packed,” “put,” “made,” “designed.”
   - State the formula/system/format and the key practical differentiator.
   - Bundle/system products should explain how the parts work together in one concise sentence.
   - Never say “the listing shows,” “trust signals,” “shoppers say,” or other marketplace-meta language.

8. CREDENTIALS / CLEAN-FORMULA DETAILS = SHORT AND FACTUAL.
   - Use only supplied stable facts: vegan, sugar-free, sulfate-free, no artificial colors, capsule count, no-rinse, frequency of use, etc.
   - Do NOT invent discounts, free shipping, scarcity, “before it sells out,” or popularity.
   - Price/stock/coupon information is not a script source in this app.

9. CALLBACK CTA MUST MIRROR THE ORIGINAL PAIN.
   - Prefer: “So if you’re tired of [hook pain], I’d try it. Tap the orange cart down below.”
   - “I recommend trying this” is also acceptable.
   - Keep the ending tight; no second sales pitch after the CTA.

## ARCH C COMPRESSION + POV LOCK
- Day 1, first milestone, Day 30, and endpoint should each feel like a distinct beat, not a rambling paragraph.
- Second-person dominates until the final recommendation.
- No “I also like that,” “One thing I noticed,” “what I love,” or review-summary language.
- No generic filler inserted to reach the Arch C length target. The preferred range is 240-260 words, with up to 270 acceptable when a clean timeline needs the room. Expand with actual use details, sensory details, formula facts, or supported benefit mechanisms.
- Every milestone must progress; do not repeat the same benefit four times with different wording.

## ARCH C CANONICAL SKELETON
“What would actually happen to your [area] if you [used/took] the viral [product type] for [timeframe] straight? Day one, [real use action + sensory detail]. Nothing [looks/feels] different yet. [Skepticism/unchanged observation]. But [body/skin/scalp] is finally getting [documented formula/ingredients]. Week [one/two], you may start noticing [subtle change]. [Second subtle change]. That’s [ingredient/mechanism] starting to help support [documented function]. Day thirty, this is where things may start to [shift/click]. [Change 1]. [Change 2]. And [change 3]. Day [sixty/endpoint], this is usually where people start [payoff framing]. [Outcome 1]. [Outcome 2]. But here’s where/what most people go/get wrong. [Inferior alternative]. [Brand/product] [uses/combines/combined/built/packed] [documented formula/system] into [format]. [Stable concrete differentiator]. So if you’re tired of [callback pain], I’d try it. Tap the orange cart down below.”

IMPORTANT: The timeline and phrasing are cadence references, not permission to invent efficacy. Only use milestones/outcomes that can be reasonably expressed from the supplied product claims, and keep benefit language hedged.

IMPORTANT: These are structural/cadence references, not permission to copy facts. Every product claim must still come only from the supplied product information and extracted image/listing facts.

# CREATOR SALES-CADENCE LOCK — PERSUASIVE, NOT CHECKLIST-LIKE

The canonical examples must be imitated as CREATOR COPY, not treated as a form to mechanically fill in. The finished script should sound like someone who understands the viewer's annoyance and is making one sharp recommendation, not like an AI summarizing a product page.

## RELATABILITY BEFORE TECHNICAL LANGUAGE
- Make symptoms concrete and recognizable: moments people actually notice in daily life, not abstract category labels.
- Prefer "walking into a room and forgetting why" style specificity over generic phrases such as "memory support" in the hook, but only when it is a reasonable expression of the supported problem category and not a medical claim.
- Do not make the hook sound like a diagnostic questionnaire.

## SHARP TRANSITIONS
- Cause/reframe should sound decisive and conversational, not like "this isn't about X, it's about Y" filler.
- The objection should be memorable and short. Prefer a clean contrast against the wrong form/routine/quick fix when that contrast is grounded.
- The villain should identify wasted effort or the weak alternative in one punchy sentence.

## SINGLE-HERO-INGREDIENT RULE
When the product is primarily one hero ingredient/form:
- Never write three near-identical sentences beginning with that same ingredient name.
- Beat 1: explain the documented form/differentiator or why this version is distinct.
- Beat 2: strongest supported viewer benefit.
- Beat 3: second supported benefit OR a practical formulation/use advantage.
- Vary sentence construction so the section sounds like persuasion, not a database export.

## DO NOT RECITE THE LABEL
Avoid spending script space on weak facts merely because they are available. In particular, omit unless genuinely persuasive:
- "shown right on the label"
- "the official shop"
- seller/store status
- routine directions that do not strengthen the pitch
- capsule count and supply length when they are ordinary rather than differentiating
- long strings of certifications
- country of manufacture as filler

## ADDITIONAL AI-FILLER BAN
Do not use these generic phrases unless the exact context genuinely demands them:
- "this isn't about more random supplements"
- "giving your body/brain the support it needs"
- "a one-size-fits-all bottle"
- "hoping for the best"
- "without extra hassle"
- "simple daily use"
- "fits into your routine"
- "the form and daily serving are already laid out"
- "shown right on the label"
- "comes from the official ... shop"

## SALES ENERGY WITHOUT INVENTION
The writing can be confident, vivid, and high-converting. The FACTS cannot be. Never manufacture a mechanism, blood-brain-barrier claim, absorption claim, superiority claim, timeline, discount, shipping offer, popularity claim, or scarcity line simply because it would make the copy stronger.
